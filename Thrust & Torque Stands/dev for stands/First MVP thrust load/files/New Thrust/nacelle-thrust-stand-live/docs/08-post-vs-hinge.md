# 08 - The Post vs the Hinge

For the conversation with Vermont. The goal is to make the argument he will find
persuasive, not to tell him his idea is bad.

## Concede the real point first

His hinge is not silly, and its error terms are mostly **systematic rather than random**.

- Arm ratio is a caliper measurement, so it is correctable.
- Tare offset is correctable.
- Bearing stiction is real but quantifiable, and small with a decent bearing.

If the question were "what is thrust at one height," his rig and this one would land within
a couple of percent of each other and there would be no strong case for either. Say that.
It is true and it is the fastest way to a productive conversation.

## The argument that actually holds

**His question is watts versus height. A hinge swings through an arc.**

On a teetering arm, height is not something you set. It is something that changes while you
measure, and it is coupled to the very variable you are trying to isolate. As the arm rises
toward balance, the rotor climbs out of ground effect mid-measurement, thrust changes,
which changes the balance point, which changes the height. The height axis smears.

The post makes height an **independent variable**. You pin it, you measure it with a tape,
you walk away. Every other quantity is then a clean function of it.

That is a design argument about experiment structure, not a claim that his rig is noisy.
It is the one to lead with.

## The secondary argument

On a lever the reading is `T x (L1 / L2)`, not `T`. Every dimensional error becomes a force
error one for one. Get the pivot 3 percent off centre and the thrust number is 3 percent
wrong, silently, with no signature in the data that would let you detect it.

The post has no arm, so it has no such term. Load is axial, and force in equals pounds out.

## What the hinge is genuinely better at

Offer this, because it is real.

A teeter is an **excellent comparator**. Hang the port and starboard nacelles on opposite
ends, run both at the same commanded throttle, and any imbalance shows up immediately as
deflection. That is a matched-pair check, and it is worth doing before the nacelles ship.

So the honest split is:

- **Post**: absolute measurement, watts vs height. The deliverable.
- **Hinge**: relative measurement, nacelle A against nacelle B. The pre-ship QC check.

Both get built. Neither is wasted.

## The compromise, if he wants his design honoured

Take his arm, replace the bathroom scale with a load cell, and **pin the arm rigidly at each
test height** instead of letting it hinge freely.

Pinned, stiction goes to zero, the height smearing goes away, and the arm ratio becomes one
measured constant applied to all readings. That is a legitimate instrument, and it is his
design. If the relationship matters more than the last two percent, this is a good answer.

## What to avoid saying

Do not lead with "your rig is noisy." The noise is not the problem and he will correctly
push back. Lead with the height axis. It is the argument that survives scrutiny.
